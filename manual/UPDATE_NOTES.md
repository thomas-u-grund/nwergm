# Update of the manual to the 1.3 series (26 October 2026)

Starting point: "Manual for version 1.2" (101 pages, built with nwergm 1.2.3, nwcommands 3.9.1). Result: "Manual for version 1.3", 104 pages, built with nwergm 1.3.0, nwcommands 3.10.0, Stata/BE 18 (macOS, native plugin), ergm 4.12.0 and R 4.6.0 (R side re-run).

## What changed in the text
- Label: `\manualseries` = 1.3 (title page, citation, ch. 1 "How to cite", `ZENODO.md`, `README.md`); `\pkgversion` 1.3.0. Installation chapter: tag example v1.3.0, core 3.6.0 or later (examples with core 3.10.0), Stata 17 or later tested with 17 and 18; new paragraph "Changes in the series 1.3".
- The Monte Carlo sampling (ch. 2, new paragraphs "Adaptive sample size and interval" and "The interplay of the options", label `sec:adaptive`; ch. 4 option tables and "The defaults follow R"; ch. 1 list of differences and of what is covered; ch. 7 on ESS and the interval printed by `estat mcmcdiag`; ch. 9 "where nwergm differs"; translation table): base sample max(256, 32 p) as `mcmcsamplesize()`, target ESS 64 as `mcmleeffectivesize()`, interval halved per iteration, thinning by two when the sample doubled, burn-in estimated from the sample, R's confidence-region convergence test, covariance and bridge from the fit's own sample, `mcmleeffectivesize(0)` = fixed-size algorithm of 1.2 (also what contrastive divergence, stochastic approximation and `obsconstraints()` always use). Deviations from R are stated (ESS inflation by the geometric mean of univariate AR spectral estimates, pooled Hotelling burn-in test, caps at 32 times the start).
- Translation table: control.ergm rows for MCMLE.burnin/interval, MCMLE.samplesize.min/per_theta, MCMLE.effectiveSize (64 or NULL).
- Speed (ch. 10): new table of the eight benchmark models, R vs nwergm 1.2.3 vs nwergm 1.3.0 (median of five fits, same time, same machine, 26 October 2026, shared 18-core Mac, load average 12 to 14; from the speed investigation of the same day, the numbers are copied, not rerun); the scaling and Lazega timing tables were rerun (load average about 6).
- Validation (ch. 9): text for the 1.3 numbers; the 20-seed comparison with R (means within 0.04 SE on five models) is quoted from the release record, it is not part of `make compare`.
- Unchanged on purpose: the AI-disclosure paragraph; no internal files or protocol numbers are cited.
- Example changes: `ex03_lazega.do` (old-settings fit now with `mcmleeffectivesize(0)`, new listings lz_interval and lz_adaptold), `ex91_compare_stata.do` and `ex9a_timing.do` (old settings with `mcmleeffectivesize(0)`; timing has a new row for the 1.2 algorithm), `ex/mklistings.py` (line wrap of the plugin path).

## Decisions for the owner
1. DOI: `\manualdoi` = 10.5281/zenodo.23661332 is still UNCONFIRMED (it may be the version DOI of the reserved draft). After publication send the all-versions ("Cite all versions") DOI, replace `\manualdoi`, rebuild, and add the DOI to the citation texts listed in `ZENODO.md`.
2. Zenodo record (`ZENODO.md`): title "Manual for version 1.3", Version 1.3.0 (explained as the series label), CC-BY-4.0, Publication / Software documentation, ORCID 0000-0001-6751-5429 copied from the nwsaom record (remove it in the form if unwanted).
3. Code, not manual: `estat mcmcdiag` prints the requested interval (`e(mcmc_interval)`), not `e(mcmc_interval_final)`; with the adaptive sample the two differ (Lazega: 1024 against 2048; with interval 50 requested, 6400), so the header can be misleading. The manual says so; a patch would print both. The advice at the end of `estat mcmcdiag` still says "increasing mcmcsamplesize()" (now the base size; `mcmleeffectivesize()` is the lever). The root `README.md` section "MCMC defaults" and its tag example (1.2.3) are stale for 1.3.0.
4. The 20-seed equivalence and the benchmark table of ch. 10 come from the release record of 1.3.0 and are not reproduced by `make compare`; if they must be, the scripts are outside this repository.
